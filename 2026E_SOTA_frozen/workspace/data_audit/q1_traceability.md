# Q1 Attachment 1 traceability

**Stage 2.5 addendum:** Direct MP4 container `mvhd` parsing recovered the official Attachment 1 duration range exactly, 2.648–34.567 s. The OpenCV frame-count discrepancy below remains a **frame decoding/timestamp** issue; it no longer blocks clip-level duration. See `workspace/evidence_recovery/timebase_evidence.md`.

`label-100.xlsx` has 100 distinct `(video_id,clip_id)` rows with transcript, continuous label and annotation. Exactly 100 video files have matching folder/file keys; there are no missing or unlabeled videos. All 100 decode at least an initial frame. OpenCV metadata gives duration 2.636–34.467 s, near the statement's 2.648–34.567 s, **but metadata frame counts disagree with sequentially decoded counts in 90/100 Attachment 1 videos** (122/140 across Attachments 1 and 4). The decoder-count/FPS duration range for Attachment 1 is 2.236–29.167 s and cannot be treated as authoritative either without an independent container/timestamp probe. This is a material timebase blocker. Label signs match annotations for all rows. Inventory, media metadata and full decode counts are saved in `workspace/results/data_audit/`.

Three clips from one source folder were inspected by sequential frame decoding; a first/middle/last-decoded-frame contact sheet was saved as `workspace/figures/data_audit/q1_frame_audit.jpg`. All three yielded frames, but decoded frame counts (163, 278, 431) were substantially below OpenCV's reported counts (261, 424, 578). Random seeking near the metadata end also failed for two. This establishes frame access, **not** a validated frame-to-second map.

What is actually supplied: transcript text at **clip level**, decodable video frames and audiovisual content in MP4. No word-level timestamp file is present. A phrase can be tied to the clip and, after separately validated alignment/manual annotation, perhaps to a time interval; it cannot now be assigned exact seconds. The finest defensible current map is `decoded frame order → candidate clip-relative time`, pending independent media timestamp verification. Audio interval timing also needs a verified decoder/sample-rate workflow; `feature position → phrase` remains unresolved. Any future Q1 extractor must log its own time bins and alignment uncertainty, retain all 100 raw keys, and avoid invented word timestamps.
