# Huawei Cup Excellent-Paper Writing Grammar — Seed

Derived from recurring structures in representative historical excellent papers. Treat as heuristics.

## Abstract

Common high-information structure:

1. one short problem/context sentence,
2. global approach sentence,
3. Question 1: difficulty -> method -> verified result,
4. Question 2: dependency/improvement -> method -> verified result,
5. remaining questions in the same compact style,
6. overall validation/robustness/practical conclusion.

Avoid spending most of the abstract on generic background.

## Main body

A recurring effective question-level structure is:

`problem analysis -> modeling rationale -> formulation -> solution -> result -> validation/discussion -> mini-summary`

## Strong transitions

Later questions often begin by stating exactly what is inherited from previous questions and what new difficulty is added.

## Method exposition

Explain standard algorithms only to the extent necessary to show:

- how they are adapted,
- what variables they operate on,
- what constraint or failure mode they solve.

## Results

Prefer concrete quantitative or decision-relevant outcomes, but only when traceable to saved evidence.

## Figures

Good figures usually have a job: model schematic, data diagnostic, baseline comparison, error validation, sensitivity, tradeoff, spatial/temporal pattern, or final decision.

## Model evaluation

Do not write generic advantages such as “high accuracy and strong robustness” unless demonstrated. Tie each claimed advantage to evidence.
